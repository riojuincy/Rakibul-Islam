About

A creator who explains, builds, and writes

My name is Rakibul Islam. The work people come for is the channel: Movie Explainer. I sit with a film long enough to know why a scene is there, then I tell that reason in a straight line. A good explanation does not rush the ending, and it does not hide the turn that makes the story matter.

Away from the channel I design complete websites, write software, and make games in C, C++, C#, and Python. I also write poetry, make graphics, edit photographs, and edit video. A page, a picture, and a cut are the same kind of problem: what stays, and what can be left out.

Work

What I make

Each kind of work has the same standard. If it has my name on it, it has been finished with care, on a phone and on a large screen alike.

01

Film explanations

This is the profession. On YouTube I am Your BD Explainer. I walk through a film so the plot, the motive, and the last scene land in the right order. The point is clarity: you should leave knowing what the story was doing.

02

Websites

Web design and the full build. I shape the pages, the type, the color, and the words a visitor actually reads. A site should feel calm in the hand and complete on a desk, with nothing left half-finished.

03

Software and games

Programs and games written in C, C++, C#, and Python. I start from the problem, then choose the structure that can carry it. Speed matters, and so does a design another person can read six months later.

04

Poetry

I write poems. The line has to be exact, the image has to be seen, and the last sentence has to earn its place. Poetry is not a side note. It is part of how I make everything else.

05

Titles, covers, and the pictures that sit beside a film or a page. Shape, color, and type have to agree, so the image can be read at a glance and still feel finished up close.

07

Video editing

I edit video. The cut, the pace, and the picture itself. A scene should move when it needs to move, and hold when the frame is already saying enough.

Writing

Poems, and the words on a page

I write poetry, and I write the language that lives on a website. Both ask for the same ear. A sentence should be plain enough to trust and shaped enough to remember. I cut until the line sounds like speech, then I leave the one detail that makes it specific.

A poem, for me, begins with something seen: a room, a road, a face in a dark screen. I do not decorate it. I stay with it until the feeling has a shape. The same habit helps a film explanation. If I cannot say the scene in one clean paragraph, I do not understand it yet.

When I write for a site, the reader should never feel lost. Headings tell the truth. Buttons say what they do. The longer passages, like this one, are there so you can slow down and actually read. That is the courtesy I want from a page, and the courtesy I try to give.
